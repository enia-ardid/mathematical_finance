A Positive Fractional--Diffusive Volatility Model\par}
\vspace{0.4cm}
{\large Enia Ardid, Pablo Casado de Amez\'ua\par}
\vspace{0.2cm}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{abstract}
We develop a conditional-variance model that weights past squared shocks
with a finite fractional kernel and pools them across assets through a
positive correlation-graph resolvent. We derive its positivity and
fixed-parameter moment properties, exact multi-step variance forecasts, and
the effective memory of a persistent asymmetric extension. The empirical study covers the
S\&P~500, eleven sector ETFs and eight country ETFs, with 186 origins per panel
during 2023--2026 and horizons of 1, 5 and 21 observations. Persistence,
asymmetry and multi-horizon selection improve the initial specification in all six US panel--horizon
comparisons. A fixed 50:50 combination with GJR-GARCH lowers pooled QLIKE
relative to the refined fractional model and FIGARCH in all nine comparisons.
Against GJR alone, it has lower loss in three; all nine paired nominal 95\%
block-bootstrap intervals include zero at both block lengths. The study
therefore establishes neither superiority nor equivalence to GJR. Diffusion
has mixed value, and the ranking against weighted Network GARCH reverses
between sectors and countries. Evaluation uses retrospective adjusted-price
snapshots downloaded in October 2026; chronological row access does not
reconstruct historical data availability.
The shared temporal kernel and graph operator make the model's impulse
response tractable, while the ablations show where these restrictions help
or hinder forecasting.
\end{abstract}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\textbf{Keywords:} fractional memory; conditional variance; graph diffusion;
sector ETFs; volatility forecasting; chronological validation.

\section{Introduction}
Volatility forecasts can draw on an asset's past shocks and on shocks to
related assets. We combine fractional temporal weighting and correlation-graph
diffusion in a conditional-variance model, with separate parameters for memory,
cross-asset smoothing and feedback.

Fractional differencing \citep{hosking1981} and FIGARCH \citep{baillie1996}
motivate the use of slowly decaying temporal weights. Our construction uses
a finite, normalized fractional-integral kernel on squared standardized
innovations. An implicit diffusion step smooths these signals over the graph
\citep{shuman2013}; a positive intercept and subunit feedback complete the recursion.

Other parsimonious volatility models distribute information across horizons.
The HAR-RV model of \citet{corsi2009} combines daily, weekly and monthly
realized-volatility components. \citet{bollerslev2016} show how accounting
for variation in measurement error can improve realized-volatility forecasts.
Our daily-close data preclude fitting these high-frequency benchmarks, but
their results motivate attention to both memory structure and proxy quality.

\subsection{Position relative to network volatility models}
Spatial ARCH permits conditional heteroskedasticity to depend on neighboring
locations \citep{otto2018}, and network log-ARCH combines temporal effects with
contemporaneous network interactions \citep{mattera2024}. Network GARCH uses
graph structure to reduce the dimension of
multivariate volatility estimation, with stationarity and QMLE results
\citep{zhou2020}. Threshold network GARCH also incorporates asymmetric shock
responses \citep{pan2024}. More recently, generalized network GARCH includes
neighboring shock and variance terms alongside covariance forecasts
\citep{zhou2026}. The contribution here is the particular restriction to a common
positive resolvent at every lag and a normalized fractional temporal profile.
This defines a restricted multivariate ARCH recursion distinct from FIGARCH.
We fit FIGARCH and first-order Network GARCH benchmarks
(Sections~\ref{sec:external}--\ref{sec:network}); network log-ARCH, threshold
Network GARCH and DCC-GNGARCH remain outside the comparison. The proofs apply
positive-filter and graph arguments to this parameterization, whose network
dependence and asymmetric responses are established ingredients.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We specify a positive fractional--diffusive variance family, establish its
fixed-parameter moment properties and derive its effective memory and exact
multi-step forecasts. We assess its restrictions through ablations, conventional
and network benchmarks, forecast combination and calibration diagnostics.
A fixed-graph bias--variance calculation identifies when diffusion reduces
signal-estimation error (Appendix~\ref{app:diffusion-risk}); a separate forward
protocol tests the mechanism on future range-based targets
(Appendix~\ref{app:forward}).
Appendix~A describes reproduction using the retained price snapshots.

\paragraph{Roadmap.}
Sections~2--6 define and evaluate the initial specification;
Section~\ref{sec:refinement} adds persistence and asymmetry;
Section~\ref{sec:external} evaluates country ETFs and additional benchmarks;
Section~9 interprets the findings, and the appendices give reproduction
details, asset paths and additional diagnostics.

\section{Data, notation and prediction target}
\subsection{Observed prices and returns}
Let $P_{t,j}>0$ denote the closing price of asset $j$ at retained trading
observation $t$, and define
\begin{equation}
r_{t,j}=\log(P_{t,j}/P_{t-1,j}).
\label{eq:returns}
\end{equation}
Write $\F_o=\sigma(P_0,\ldots,P_o)$ for the information generated by observations
through origin $o$ in a fixed data vintage. All transformations, graph estimates
and parameter choices used to forecast from $o$ depend only on these rows.
For the empirical study, this ordering refers to the downloaded snapshot;
it does not establish which versions of those rows were available on date $o$.

The market snapshot was downloaded on 17 October 2026 from Yahoo Finance through
\texttt{yfinance} 0.2.66, with adjusted prices and an exclusive request end date
of 17 October 2026. It contains the index \texttt{\^{}GSPC} and the eleven ETFs
XLB, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLRE, XLU, XLV, XLY and XLC. After inception trimming
and alignment, common prices run from 19 June 2018 through 16 October 2026.
Prices must be positive, finite, uniquely dated and increasing in date; missing
interior observations are not filled.

The index and ETF panel are fitted separately. Adjusted ETF returns and an index
price-return series represent different economic quantities. The index is
therefore a separate forecasting series, rather than a self-financing
replication by the sector funds. Section~\ref{sec:vintages} explains how
price revisions can affect this retrospective evaluation.

\subsection{Risk target and common mean convention}
At each parameter refit, estimate a mean $\mu_j$ and a positive scale $s_j^2$
from the observed fitting window. These estimates are shared by all benchmark
models until the next refit. Write
\begin{equation}
u_{t,j}=r_{t,j}-\mu_j,\qquad z_{t,j}=u_{t,j}^2/s_j^2.
\end{equation}
The $h$-observation evaluation target is
\begin{equation}
Y_{o,h,j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{h}(r_{o+\ell,j}-\mu_j)^2,
\qquad h\in\{1,5,21\}.
\label{eq:proxy}
\end{equation}
The target uses daily returns. Under the specified constant conditional mean,
it is conditionally unbiased for the sum of future conditional variances.
A misspecified mean adds squared mean error to that expectation. The empirical
task therefore measures forecasts of centered return second moments.
Intraday realized variance aggregates high-frequency returns and has its own
measurement-error theory \citep{barndorff2002}; our daily squared-return proxy
does not provide that richer measurement.

\section{A positive fractional--diffusive model}
\subsection{Finite fractional-integral memory}
For $0<d\le1$, the binomial expansion of $(1-B)^{-d}$ yields positive coefficients
\begin{equation}
g_0(d)=1,\qquad
g_k(d)=\frac{k-1+d}{k}\,g_{k-1}(d)
=\frac{\Gamma(k+d)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(k+1)}.
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
With memory length $M$, normalize these coefficients:
\begin{equation}
p_k(d)=\frac{g_k(d)}{\sum_{v=0}^{M-1}g_v(d)},\quad
m_t=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}p_k(d)z_{t-1-k}.
\label{eq:memory}
\end{equation}
The weights satisfy $p_k>0$ and $\sum_kp_k=1$. For large $k$, $g_k(d)$ behaves as
$k^{d-1}/\Gamma(d)$. Within a fixed window, increasing $d$ spreads weight toward
older observations; $d=1$ gives a uniform moving average. The study fixes
$M=252$. It does not infer $d$ from a Hurst estimator.

The normalized operator is a finite-window analogue of a fractional integral.
For a smooth signal $q$, a physical window $T$ and a refining observation step,
the corresponding normalized fractional integral is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_{d,T}q(t)=\frac{d}{T^d}\int_0^T a^{d-1}q(t-a)\,da.
\end{equation}
This relation motivates the kernel; the empirical model itself is the finite
discrete recursion \eqref{eq:memory}. Finite memory and subunit feedback do not
establish asymptotic long-range dependence or convergence to a fractional
stochastic PDE.

\subsection{Correlation graph and implicit diffusion}
Estimate a Ledoit--Wolf covariance matrix \citep{ledoit2004} after standardizing
the fitting-window returns. Convert it to a correlation matrix $R$ and set
\begin{equation}
W_{ij}=\max(R_{ij},0)^2\quad(i\ne j),\qquad W_{ii}=0.
\end{equation}
Let $D=\diag(W\one)$ and rescale the graph Laplacian by its mean degree:
\begin{equation}
L=\frac{D-W}{\max(J^{-1}\operatorname{tr}D,10^{-12})},\qquad
A_\tau=(I+\tau L)^{-1},\quad\tau\ge0.
\label{eq:diffusion}
\end{equation}
For the scalar index, $L=0$ and $A_\tau=1$; index results contain no evidence about
cross-sector diffusion. For sectors, the update $x=A_\tau m$ is the implicit
Euler step $x-m=-\tau Lx$ for graph heat diffusion. The diffusion parameter
controls smoothing on this estimated network, not a causal contagion rate.

No arbitrary one-dimensional sector ordering is required. Under a permutation
matrix $\Pi$, the same construction transforms $L$ into $\Pi L\Pi^{\mathsf T}$
and $A_\tau$ into $\Pi A_\tau\Pi^{\mathsf T}$. Forecasts inherit this
permutation equivariance.

\subsection{Conditional variance and return generation}
For $0\le\eta<1$, define normalized conditional variance $h_t$ and asset-scale
variance $v_t$ by
\begin{equation}
h_t=(1-\eta)\one+\eta A_\tau m_t,\qquad
v_t=s^2\odot h_t,
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes elementwise multiplication. Returns obey
\begin{equation}
r_t=\mu+\diag(\sqrt{v_t})\,\varepsilon_t,
\quad \E[\varepsilon_t\mid\F_{t-1}]=0,\quad
\E[\varepsilon_{t,j}^2\mid\F_{t-1}]=1.
\label{eq:returnmodel}
\end{equation}
Simulations use temporally independent Gaussian innovation vectors with
correlation $R$; Gaussianity is not required by the marginal moment results.

Diffusion acts on standardized variance signals. It preserves their sum, not
necessarily the sum of variances in original asset units. This distinction
allows sectors to retain different volatility scales.

To make the connection with multivariate ARCH explicit, let
$S=\diag(s_1^2,\ldots,s_J^2)$. In asset units, the lag coefficient matrices are
$C_k=\eta p_k S A_\tau S^{-1}$, giving
\[
v_t=(1-\eta)s^2+
\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}C_k\bigl(u_{t-1-k}\odot u_{t-1-k}\bigr).
\]
All $C_k$ are nonnegative, and
$\rho(\sum_k C_k)=\eta$. Fractional weighting and graph diffusion constrain
these matrices to an interpretable shared structure.

\section{Mathematical properties}
\begin{proposition}[Positive, nonamplifying graph diffusion]
For a symmetric nonnegative adjacency matrix and $\tau\ge0$, $A_\tau$ is
entrywise nonnegative and doubly stochastic. Its eigenvalues lie in $(0,1]$.
For any real vector $x$,
\begin{equation}
\min_jx_j\le(A_\tau x)_i\le\max_jx_j,\qquad
(A_\tau x)^{\mathsf T}L(A_\tau x)\le x^{\mathsf T}Lx.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Laplacian is symmetric positive semidefinite and $L\one=0$. Hence
$I+\tau L$ is invertible and $A_\tau\one=\one$. For $b\ge0$, a negative minimum
of the solution to $x+\tau Lx=b$ would make the corresponding left-hand component
negative, a contradiction. Hence $A_\tau\ge0$. Symmetry gives unit column sums.
The componentwise bound follows from convex averaging. Diagonalizing $L$ gives
eigenvalues $(1+\tau\lambda_i)^{-1}$ for $A_\tau$; the energy inequality follows
from $\lambda_i/(1+\tau\lambda_i)^2\le\lambda_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positivity and fixed-parameter moment stability]
Fix $M<\infty$, $s_j^2>0$ and $0\le\eta<1$. For nonnegative observed squared
innovations, $v_{t,j}\ge(1-\eta)s_j^2$. If the innovation vectors are iid over time
with zero means and unit marginal second moments, the normalized squared-return
recursion admits a unique causal stationary solution with finite first moments,
and its stationary marginal expectations satisfy $\E z_t=\one$. It follows that
the corresponding stationary returns have variance $s_j^2$ and finite second
moments $\mu_j^2+s_j^2$.
\label{prop:stability}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound follows from nonnegative weights and nonnegative diffusion.
If initial component expectations are bounded by $C\ge1$, then
$\E h_{t,j}\le(1-\eta)+\eta C\le C$, and $\E z_{t,j}=\E h_{t,j}$.
For two histories driven by the same innovation sequence, put
$e_t=\E\lVert z_t-z_t'\rVert_1$. Independence of the current innovations from the
past, unit marginal second moments and the unit column sums of $A_\tau$ give
\begin{equation}
e_t\le\eta\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}p_ke_{t-1-k}.
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
The largest expected discrepancy contracts by at least $\eta$ each time an
entire block of $M$ initial lags is replaced. Backward iteration from a bounded
deterministic history is therefore Cauchy in $L^1$, yielding a causal stationary
limit; the same bound gives uniqueness among finite-first-moment solutions.
In stationarity, $b=\E z_t$ solves
$b=(1-\eta)\one+\eta A_\tau b$. Since $\rho(\eta A_\tau)<1$, its unique solution
is $b=\one$.
\end{proof}

The moment result concerns the fixed-parameter recursion, rather than the
sequence produced by rolling estimation. It supplies finite return second
moments; fourth moments and a limit distribution for empirical losses would
require additional conditions. Finite-window contraction also distinguishes
the fractional shape of the weights from asymptotic long-range dependence.

\section{Forecasting and estimation}
\subsection{Exact multi-step marginal moments}
At origin $o$, let $\bar z_{o+\ell\mid o}$ equal observed $z$ for nonfuture lags,
and recursively replace future squared innovations by their conditional
expectations:
\begin{equation}
\bar h_{o+\ell\mid o}
=(1-\eta)\one+\eta A_\tau
\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}p_k\bar z_{o+\ell-1-k\mid o},\qquad
\bar z_{o+\ell\mid o}=\bar h_{o+\ell\mid o}.
\end{equation}
Linearity and unit innovation second moments make this recursion exact under
the fitted variance model. The aggregate forecast is
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_{o,h}=s^2\odot\sum_{\ell=1}^{h}\bar h_{o+\ell\mid o}.
\label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
With the model's conditional mean, centered increments are martingale
differences, so \eqref{eq:aggregate} also gives the marginal conditional
variance of cumulative future log-returns. Future variances remain random,
however, and their sum does not give a Gaussian predictive law for returns.
Our forecasts target variance directly.

\subsection{Chronological selection}
Forecast origins occur every five retained observations on a grid beginning at
price position 252; scoring begins in 2023. At the first scored origin and whenever
at least 21 observations have elapsed since the last refit, parameters are
updated. This gives a 25-observation refit spacing on the five-observation grid,
with 38 refits per panel in the reported experiment.

At a refit origin, reserve the final 252 observed returns for validation. Means,
scales and graph structure used to compute validation predictions are estimated
strictly before that block, using at most 1,008 returns. As validation advances,
already observed squared returns may update the memory state. Their future
counterparts are never accessed. Select the minimum pooled one-step QLIKE over
\begin{align}
d&\in\{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8\}, &\eta&\in\{0.8,0.95,0.99\},\\
\tau&\in\{0,0.25,1,4\}.&&
\end{align}
The index fixes $\tau=0$. Stable candidate order resolves exact ties. After
selection, refit means, scales and the graph on the last 1,008 available returns,
including validation observations, and forecast subsequent targets.
The historical period was available during earlier research iterations, so
this chronological evaluation remains a development study.

The selected parameters serve all three horizons, although selection minimizes
only one-step loss. That mismatch motivates the multi-horizon refinement in
Section~\ref{sec:refinement}. All methods are scored at the same origins,
excluding end blocks without complete targets for every horizon.

\subsection{Benchmarks and ablations}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Historical variance:} the current fitting-window sample variance,
held constant over each forecast horizon.
\item \textbf{EWMA:} squared-innovation averaging with decay selected from
$\{0.9,0.94,0.97,0.99\}$ on the same validation block. Its future conditional
variance remains constant under its own update equation.
\item \textbf{GARCH(1,1):} independent asset models estimated by Gaussian
quasi-maximum likelihood \citep{bollerslev1986}. Positivity is imposed and
$\alpha+\beta<0.999$. Two fixed initializations are used; all 456 selected
asset-level fits report convergence.
\item \textbf{Fractional only:} the proposed kernel and feedback with $\tau=0$,
with its own validation-selected $d$ and $\eta$.
\item \textbf{Exponential plus graph:} replace the fractional average by EWMA
while retaining positive feedback and graph diffusion. Its decay, feedback and
diffusion grids have the same candidate count as the fractional graph model.
\end{enumerate}

Each ablation selects its remaining parameters on its own validation losses.

\section{Evaluation of the initial specification}
\subsection{Loss and dependence}
The primary score is a normalized QLIKE loss,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{o,h,j}=\log\left(\frac{\widehat V_{o,h,j}}{h s_j^2}\right)
+\frac{Y_{o,h,j}}{\widehat V_{o,h,j}}.
\label{eq:qlike}
\end{equation}
The normalization is known at the origin and common to all methods. It changes
the level, not paired comparisons. QLIKE is appropriate for conditional
second-moment forecasting with noisy, conditionally unbiased proxies
\citep{patton2011,patton2009}. Scores can be negative, and ratios of score levels are not reported as
percentage improvements. A normalized squared-error diagnostic is also retained
in the machine-readable results.

There are 186 common origins from 9 January 2023 to 17 September 2026, with
targets through 16 October 2026. The sector panel contributes 2,046 asset--origin
pairs per horizon, which must not be treated as independent replications.
Twenty-one-observation targets overlap. Paired circular block-bootstrap ranges
use 2,000 replicates, seed 1729, and blocks of five or ten origins. Entire sector
vectors are resampled together. Each replicate averages the paired loss
difference, with negative values favoring the proposed forecast. The 2.5th
and 97.5th percentiles give the reported nominal 95\% intervals.
This resampling preserves dependence within each block and across assets;
it does not adjust for model selection, multiple comparisons, nonstationarity
or previous researcher exposure. An interval containing zero leaves the
direction unresolved under this procedure; it is not an equivalence test.
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} adds paired predictive-accuracy tests following
\citet{diebold1995} and calibration regressions, with dependence-aware
covariance estimates. The statistical interpretation in this paragraph
applies to all subsequent loss comparisons.

\section{Persistence and asymmetric innovations}
\label{sec:refinement}
\subsection{Specification and moment properties}
The initial model responds identically to positive and negative innovations and
selects parameters using only one-step losses. We retain its fractional kernel
and graph diffusion while allowing a persistent variance state and asymmetric
shock contributions. Define
\begin{align}
x_{t,j}&=z_{t,j}\frac{1+\delta\mathbf1\{u_{t,j}<0\}}{1+\delta/2},\\
h_t&=(1-b)(1-\eta)\one+b h_{t-1}
 +(1-b)\eta A_\tau\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}p_k x_{t-1-k},
\label{eq:refined}
\end{align}
with $v_t=s^2\odot h_t$, $0\le b<1$ and $\delta\ge0$. The parameter $b$
smooths the variance response. With $\delta=1$, a negative innovation has twice
the contribution of a positive innovation of the same magnitude.

For the asymmetric recursion, assume conditionally symmetric innovations with
unit second moments. Conditional symmetry strengthens the moment assumptions:
symmetry gives $\E[\varepsilon_{t,j}^2\mathbf1\{\varepsilon_{t,j}<0\}]=1/2$
and therefore $\E[x_t\mid\F_{t-1}]=h_t$. Gaussian simulation innovations satisfy
the assumption; it is not asserted as an empirical property of market shocks.
Future expected variances are obtained by the same substitution as in
Section~5.1, retaining the additional state $h_{t-1}$.

Unit initial variance and unit presample transformed squares give the floor
$v_{t,j}\ge(1-\eta)s_j^2$ by induction. For fixed parameters and iid symmetric
innovations, the shared-noise argument for Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}
now gives, for discrepancies between normalized variance processes,
\begin{equation}
e_t\le b e_{t-1}+(1-b)\eta\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}p_k e_{t-1-k},\qquad
b+(1-b)\eta<1.
\end{equation}
The maximum expected discrepancy over the finite lag state contracts over
successive $M$-observation blocks. The same backward-iteration argument yields
a unique causal finite-first-moment stationary variance process. Its mean is
$\one$, so return variance is $s_j^2$. The recursive state adds exponentially
decaying persistence; it does not establish asymptotic long-range dependence.

\subsection{Effective memory and graph modes}
The persistence state has an exact filter interpretation. For fixed parameters,
unroll \eqref{eq:refined} after removing its initialization transient to obtain
\begin{align}
h_t&=(1-\eta)\one+\eta A_\tau\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\widetilde p_jx_{t-j},\\
\widetilde p_j&=(1-b)\sum_{k=0}^{\min(M-1,j-1)}p_k b^{j-1-k}.
\label{eq:effectivekernel}
\end{align}
Indeed, repeated substitution expands the state into a geometric sum; grouping
terms with the same innovation lag yields \eqref{eq:effectivekernel}. Since
all terms are nonnegative, summing in either order gives
$\sum_{j\ge1}\widetilde p_j=(1-b)\sum_kp_k\sum_{\ell\ge0}b^\ell=1$.
For $0<b<1$ and $j\ge M$, $\widetilde p_{j+1}=b\widetilde p_j$.
At $b=0$ the weights reduce to $p_{j-1}$ for $1\le j\le M$, with zero tail.
The refinement geometrically smooths the finite fractional kernel; its
infinite tail, when present, is exponential rather than hyperbolic.

With $L=U\Lambda U^{\mathsf T}$, the graph-mode coefficient at lag $j$ is
$\eta\widetilde p_j/(1+\tau\lambda_\ell)$. Temporal weighting and spatial
smoothing are separable: every lag shares the same graph attenuation. This
clarifies both interpretability and a restriction relative to more general
network lag matrices. A numerical impulse-response test verifies the identity
against the implemented filtering recursion.

A follow-up benchmark evaluates the first-order recursion of
\citet{zhou2020}, equation (2.2), on the same sector and country panels:
\begin{equation}
h^{N}_t=\omega\one+\alpha z_{t-1}+\lambda Bz_{t-1}+\beta h^{N}_{t-1},
\qquad \omega>0,\quad \alpha,\lambda,\beta\ge0,\quad
\alpha+\lambda+\beta<0.999.
\label{eq:network-benchmark}
\end{equation}
Here $B$ row-normalizes the positive squared correlation weights already used
to construct the fractional model's graph. Its diagonal and isolated rows are
zero. A sensitivity benchmark replaces these weights by binary edges at
$R_{ij}\ge0.5$ before normalization; a pooled GARCH control sets $\lambda=0$.
The coefficients are shared across the standardized assets. These choices
adapt the published recursion to estimated correlation networks rather than
the original shareholder network. They do not reproduce the original paper's
empirical application or its assumptions of an exogenous network.

preceding 1,008 returns, the common fitted means and scales, and initial
normalized variance one. Three fixed starting points are optimized with
analytic gradients; the fit with the lowest finite training objective is
retained. Observed returns update the state between refits. The first future
variance follows \eqref{eq:network-benchmark}; subsequent conditional
expectations replace future $z_t$ by its conditional mean $h^N_t$. Daily
forecasts are summed over the original horizons and evaluated on identical
targets. All 228 selected fits and all 684 optimization starts converge.

\section{Interpretation, limitations and conclusions}
\subsection{What the restrictions explain}
\textbf{Theoretical contribution.}
The model restricts multivariate variance lag matrices to a common positive
graph resolvent and a shared fractional profile. This structure yields an
explicit impulse response and fixed-parameter moment bounds. It excludes
asset-specific lag shapes and lag-dependent network geometry. The persistent
state smooths the finite fractional profile geometrically, producing an
infinite exponential tail when its coefficient is positive.

\textbf{Empirical contribution.}
Persistence and asymmetry improve the initial model, while the benefits of
diffusion and fractional weighting vary across panels and horizons.
Combining the model with GJR lowers loss relative to its standalone forecasts
and FIGARCH, but establishes neither superiority nor equivalence to GJR.
Table~\ref{tab:findings} summarizes the comparisons. The calibration diagnostics
also reveal one-session country-panel miscalibration for all three examined
models, including GJR (Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}).

\subsection{Data vintages and prospective evaluation}
\label{sec:vintages}
Chronological access to a retrospective snapshot does not recover historical
data availability. For prices from two download vintages, define
$a_t=P_t^{(T^{\prime})}/P_t^{(T)}>0$. Their log-returns satisfy
\begin{equation}
r_t^{(T^{\prime})}-r_t^{(T)}
=\log a_t-\log a_{t-1}.
\label{eq:vintage-return}
\end{equation}
A common multiplicative adjustment cancels; changes in adjacent-date factors
can alter returns, fitted parameters and targets. The data interface documents
possible dividend, split and price corrections \citep{yfinance_repair}, without
establishing their occurrence in these panels. With only one retained vintage
per panel, we cannot measure this sensitivity.

Appendix~\ref{app:forward} fixes a separate future evaluation with archived
data vintages, forecasts issued before their target intervals, range-based
volatility measurements and explicit failure rules. Its results are pending.

\subsection{Scope and reproducibility}
Daily squared-return targets are noisy, the conditional mean is constant
within each fit, and the universes share market shocks. The code and records
permit checks against the equations given the retained inputs. Prospective
observations and richer variance measurements are needed to strengthen the
evidence; network log-ARCH, threshold Network GARCH and dynamic-covariance
benchmarks would test less restrictive dependence structures.

\section{Relation to the original model}
The original project used signed Gr\"unwald--Letnikov coefficients in a return
recurrence and a reflected nearest-neighbor sector Laplacian. Isolating the
current value in a GL derivative does not yield that recurrence's lag and sign
convention. A fractional derivative also does not inherit the ordinary
logarithmic chain rule. The present work therefore starts from
\eqref{eq:weights}--\eqref{eq:returnmodel}, rather than invoking a fractional
Black--Scholes equation.

The retained idea is memory plus diffusion. The changes are explicit: positive
fractional-integral weights act on squared standardized innovations; a symmetric
estimated graph replaces the artificial sector lattice; positive feedback
controls second moments; and risk forecasts are compared chronologically against
strong variance references. Hurst identification, convergence of a pricing PDE,
and directional price-prediction superiority are not claimed.

\section{When diffusion reduces estimation error}
\label{app:diffusion-risk}
The graph can suppress measurement noise while also erasing genuine differences
between assets. The following calculation separates these effects. It concerns
squared-error estimation of a latent signal with a fixed graph; it is not a
claim that the fitted forecasting procedure improves QLIKE. Finite measurement-noise
covariance is an additional assumption: second moments of returns alone
do not ensure finite variance of squared-return measurements.

Condition on information $\mathcal G$ under which a symmetric graph Laplacian
$L$, a signal $m\in\R^J$ and a finite noise covariance $\Sigma$ are fixed. Suppose
$x=m+\varepsilon$, with $\E[\varepsilon\mid\mathcal G]=0$ and
$\E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^\top\mid\mathcal G]=\Sigma$. Write
$A_\tau=(I+\tau L)^{-1}$ and
$R_\tau=\E[\|A_\tau x-m\|_2^2\mid\mathcal G]$.

\begin{proposition}[Bias and noise reduction under a fixed graph]
\label{prop:diffusion-risk}
For $\tau\geq0$,
\begin{align}
R_\tau&=\|(A_\tau-I)m\|_2^2+
            \operatorname{tr}(A_\tau\Sigma A_\tau),\\
R_\tau-R_0&=\|(A_\tau-I)m\|_2^2-
            \operatorname{tr}\{(I-A_\tau^2)\Sigma\}.
\label{eq:diffusion-risk}
\end{align}
Consequently, diffusion improves total squared-error risk precisely when its
squared bias is smaller than its reduction of noise variance.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand $A_\tau x-m=(A_\tau-I)m+A_\tau\varepsilon$. The conditional cross term
vanishes. The noise term is $\operatorname{tr}(A_\tau\Sigma A_\tau)$ by symmetry
of $A_\tau$. Subtract $R_0=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)$ and use cyclicity of trace.
The spectrum of $A_\tau$ lies in $(0,1]$, so the noise reduction is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

Let $Lv_k=\lambda_kv_k$, with orthonormal $v_k$, and define
$\theta_k=v_k^\top m$, $\nu_k=v_k^\top\Sigma v_k$ and
$a_k=(1+\tau\lambda_k)^{-1}$. The same difference is
\begin{equation}
R_\tau-R_0=\sum_{k=1}^J\left\{
 (1-a_k)^2\theta_k^2-(1-a_k^2)\nu_k\right\}.
\label{eq:spectral-risk}
\end{equation}
This identity does not require $L$ and $\Sigma$ to commute. Modes with
$\lambda_k=0$ are unchanged. For $\tau\lambda_k>0$, smoothing improves the
individual mode precisely when
\[
\theta_k^2<\left(1+\frac{2}{\tau\lambda_k}\right)\nu_k.
\]
The sum in \eqref{eq:spectral-risk}, rather than improvement of every mode,
is the necessary and sufficient aggregate condition. At zero strength,
$R'_0=-2\operatorname{tr}(L\Sigma)$: sufficiently small positive diffusion
helps if this trace is positive. At large strength, the estimator approaches
projection onto the graph nullspace and may retain substantial bias.

For a fixed intercept and previous state, the one-step forcing
$c\one+b h+aA_\tau x$ estimates $c\one+b h+am$ with risk $a^2R_\tau$.
The statement does not compare recursively refitted processes with different
past states. Nor does it automatically apply when $L$ is estimated from the
same noisy observations: conditioning on that graph need not preserve
zero-mean noise. A graph estimated on an earlier block or an explicitly
specified conditional noise model would be needed for that interpretation.
If the target itself contains noise correlated with $\varepsilon$, the
forecast-risk comparison has an additional covariance term. These restrictions
separate the mechanism calculation from an empirical performance guarantee.

Figure~\ref{fig:diffusion-risk} checks the identity in a positive-signal
experiment. An eight-node cycle has edge weights $1/2$, with
$m_j=1+q(-1)^j$ and independent measurements
$x_j=m_j\chi^2_{24}/24$. Hence $\Sigma=\diag(2m_j^2/24)$.
The two fixed amplitudes $q=0.05$ and $q=0.8$ represent similar and
heterogeneous signals. At $\tau=1$, exact total risk changes from $0.668333$
to $0.266239$ in the first case and from $1.093333$ to $2.696557$ in the
second. The calculation uses 40,000 draws with seed 20261026 and common random
numbers across strengths. All plotted risk differences agree with their
analytic values within six Monte Carlo standard errors. These are synthetic
estimation results, not new market forecast scores.

\section{Frozen forward evaluation}
\label{app:forward}
A separate protocol fixes future evaluation rules before its first target
window. It retains the model and selection grids in the main study and adds
an OHLC-based measurement of volatility. No completed forward evaluation is
reported here. The public protocol, code hashes and calendar are supplied as
\texttt{prospective\_protocol.json}; collection records are kept separately
from all historical experiments.

\paragraph{Forecasts and comparisons.}
The scheduled design uses 52 origins five exchange sessions apart, beginning
on 27 October 2026, and 25-session refits. One panel retains the eleven sector
ETFs; a second comprises AGG, IEF, TLT, TIP, GLD, SLV, DBC and VNQ, extending
the asset mix to bonds, metals, commodities and real estate. Universe membership
reflects asset classes rather than measured forecasting gains. These panels
remain exposed to common market shocks.

The fractional model, kernel grids, 252-observation validation, 1008-observation
estimation window and mean/scale conventions are unchanged. Freezing refers to
the specification and update rules; fitted coefficients may change at the
scheduled refits. In addition to the selected model and conventional references,
matched counterfactuals set only diffusion to $0$, $0.25$, $1$ or $4$, retaining
the selected kernel, feedback, persistence, asymmetry, mean and scale. This
isolates the effect of diffusion from reselection of other parameters.

An evening forecast must be archived by 23:59:59 New York time at the origin.
Because an overnight return starts at the preceding close, the design excludes
the immediately following session from the target: an $h$-session forecast is
$\sum_{k=2}^{h+1}\widehat v_{o+k\mid o}$ for $h\in\{1,5,21\}$.
The first target interval therefore starts at the next session's close,
after issuance. This one-session embargo is explicit; these scores must not be
pooled with the historical study's immediate-next-session targets.

\paragraph{Volatility measurements.}
For consistently adjusted daily open, high, low and close prices
$(O_t,H_t,L_t,C_t)$, define
\begin{align}
g_t&=\log(O_t/C_{t-1}),\\
\operatorname{RS}_t&=
 \log(H_t/O_t)\log(H_t/C_t)+
 \log(L_t/O_t)\log(L_t/C_t),\\
Y^{\mathrm{range}}_{o,h,j}&=
 \sum_{k=2}^{h+1}\left\{
 (g_{o+k,j}-\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{gap}}_{o,j})^2+
 \operatorname{RS}_{o+k,j}\right\}.
\end{align}
The gap mean is estimated on the common trailing estimation window at refit
and then held fixed. The Rogers--Satchell term estimates intraday variance
under a log-Brownian model with drift \citep{rogers1991}; observed discrete
extrema and departures from that model can introduce measurement error.
The sum with the centered overnight square estimates whole-day variance only
with suitable conditional centering and no material overnight/intraday
covariance. It is not high-frequency realized variance and is not asserted
to be a universally unbiased proxy for the existing model. The centered
close-to-close squared-return target remains a prespecified sensitivity
measure, using exactly the same forecasts and dates. Neither OHLC target
is used to tune the model on the forward evaluation.

\paragraph{Data capture and inference.}
The first complete same-session snapshot received between 17:00 and 23:59:59
New York time is retained with raw prices, adjustment factors recoverable from
raw and adjusted closes, request/reception times and hashes. Later corrections
do not replace the first capture. Missing sessions never shorten a horizon;
failed fits, late forecasts and absent snapshots remain in the coverage record.
A missed scheduled refit invalidates its dependent origins until the next
scheduled refit. No automatic substitute model is used.

Two primary contrasts are fixed in each panel: matched $\tau=1$ versus
$\tau=0$ on the five-session range target, and selected fractional versus
GJR on the 21-session range target. The four primary tests use two-sided
DM statistics with Bartlett HAC lag four and Holm adjustment at 5\%; lag
nine is a fixed sensitivity. Formal primary tests require all 52 scheduled
matched origins, preventing compressed time gaps from entering the HAC
calculation. Incomplete coverage produces descriptive scores and an
inconclusive primary test. Complete coverage does not itself establish
adequate power. Other strengths, horizons and the close-based target are
secondary analyses. Reporting follows the fixed last target, without interim
performance rankings or outcome-dependent stopping. Local timestamps and
hashes support an audit but do not by themselves constitute independent
preregistration or external proof of forecast publication time.
